One request, start to finish

What happens when you say “Athena, open Spotify”

You speak→Microphone→Whisper: sound to text→Was “Athena” said?→Agent→Shortcut or AI model→Tool→She answers

  1. The microphone hears you. voice_mic.py records one sentence. If you hold Right Alt, it records exactly while the key is down, even over music.

  2. Whisper turns the sound into text on your graphics card, in about half a second. voice_boot.py loads it when the voice program starts.

  3. She checks you meant her. voice_text.py makes sure “Athena” opens the sentence and that it wasn't noise, a video or her own voice. If not, the sentence is ignored.

  4. The agent decides what to do. agent.py first tries the shortcuts in routes*.py. “Open Spotify” is a clear command, so a shortcut sends it straight to the open_app tool. No AI model is needed, which is why it's fast.

  5. If no shortcut fits (“what's a good song for studying?”), the agent asks an AI model through llm.py and cloud.py. registry.py shows the model only the few tools that fit the question, and it either answers or picks a tool.

  6. Anything risky waits for your yes. Deleting a file, sending a message or clicking a button on a website: agent_safety.py makes her ask first.

  7. The tool runs. Here system_apps.py finds Spotify and opens it.

  8. She answers. voice_speaker.py says it out loud in her Kokoro voice, and the Stage panel in the corner shows a card (stage.py).

Typing on the phone app or texting on Telegram skips the first three steps and starts at step 4, inside the web program.

What's running

The six programs on your PC

Athena isn't one program. Several small ones run side by side and start by themselves when you log in to Windows.

Webrun.py web
The hub, always running. Serves the HUD and the phone app, answers typed chat and Telegram, and restarts the voice program if it ever stops.
Voicerun.py voice
Her ears and mouth. Started by the web program.
Stagerun.py stage
The small floating panel in the bottom-right corner while you talk to her.
Hotkeyrun.py hotkey
Press Ctrl+Alt+Space anywhere to type her a command.
HUD windowrun.py gui
The dashboard in its own window on the PC.
Deckpythonw -m deck
The board of AI terminals. Runs only while you have it open.

Vocabulary

Words you'll keep seeing

Tool
One action Athena can take, such as opening an app or setting the volume. She has 192.
Shortcut
A fixed rule that sends a clear command straight to a tool, without the AI model. Fast and predictable. In the code these are called routes.
AI model
The language model that understands free-form requests. Hers is NVIDIA's Nemotron online, with Groq as a backup and a small model on your PC through Ollama.
Agent
The part that connects the AI model to the tools: asks the model, runs what it picks, and repeats until the job is done.
Whisper
The speech-to-text model that runs on your graphics card.
Kokoro
Her voice: the model that turns text into speech.
Wake word
“Athena”. She acts on what you say only after hearing it, or while you hold Right Alt, or when she has just asked you a question.
HUD
Her dashboard, on the PC and the phone.
Stage
The floating panel showing your words as you speak and what she did.
Deck
A board of real AI terminals (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini) with notes and checklists.
Mission
A big job, like building a website, done in the background by Claude or Gemini.
Routine, skill
A name plus a list of steps. Say the name and every step runs.
Rule, loop
Automation she runs by herself: “when this happens, do that”, or “keep doing this until it's done”.
Plug-in
A ready-made outside tool set she can use, such as the hidden browser or GitHub. Also called an MCP server.
Key, token
A secret code that lets Athena use a service. Yours are in Windows Credential Manager, not in any file.
Test
A small program that checks she still works after a change.

Doing things yourself

If you want to…

Change her voice
Edit the voice section of config.yaml, then restart her.
Add a routine
Add a name and its steps to routines.yaml. Or after she finishes a job, say “save that as morning setup”.
Save a new key
Run add_keys.py in PowerShell and type the provider's name.
See what went wrong
Run run.py failures, or read data\voice.log.
Check nothing broke
Run the files in the tests folder. Each prints how many checks failed, which should be 0.
Undo a change
Every pushed version is on GitHub, so any file can be brought back.
